Six playable sketches

Rhythm games that aren't about hitting falling notes

Each key below is a rough prototype of one idea. Turn your sound on and pick one. Everything works with touch, and with a keyboard where noted.

Compose, don't follow

Loop Lock

The runner crosses one tile per step and reacts to whatever sound you put on that step. Build a loop that gets it to the end of the path and the door opens. Open ground is free, so add notes there to make the beat your own.

  • Wall needs a kick
  • Pit needs a snare
  • Beam needs a hat
  • zZ needs silence

You are the tempo

Baton

Each tap is one beat, and the band plays exactly when you tap. The dashed line is the tempo the score asks for. Keep your dots inside the shaded band. Land 75% of the beats to pass a level. There are ten scores, and you can try them in any order.

Play the silence

Dead Air

You hear four beats, then the track cuts out and nothing on screen moves. Keep counting in your head and tap on the ringed beat, where the music comes back in. The silence gets longer each round.

    Rhythm as a movement rule

    Kick Patrol

    You can only step on a beat, one step per beat. Red guards watch their whole lane on the kick (beats 1 and 3). Yellow guards watch on the snare (2 and 4). Step into a lane on the beat its guard is not looking, and step out on the very next beat. Reach the exit.

    Arrow keys or WASD also move.

    Two rhythms at once

    Three on Four

    Your left hand plays 3 evenly spaced hits per bar while your right hand plays 4. They only meet on the first beat. Hit every circle as the line crosses it. A bar counts as clean when you land all seven hits with no extras.

    Beat deckbuilder

    Bar Fight

    Each turn is one bar of 8 beats. Cards cost beats instead of mana, so a card is as wide as the time it takes. The Metronome shows which beats it will hit on. Arrange your bar around that, then play it.
